## New Homepage Candidates

1. **Today board** — a seven-day strip plus addable, checkable tasks for the selected day. Save locally and show only three tasks at a time. References: [Tweloc](https://tweloc.com/), [DayWeaver demo](https://day-weaver.com/demo).
2. **Time-block ribbon** — drag three small task pills into a simple vertical day timeline; blocks snap into place and can be resized. Reference: [ToolsOnDuty Daily Planner](https://toolsonduty.com/daily-planner).
3. **Now / Next / Later triage** — drop a task card into one of three columns; counts update instantly. It is a deliberately tiny Kanban, not Trello rebuilt. **Selected.** Reference: [Kanboard live demo](https://kanboard.io/kanban-board-online).
4. **Focus sprint** — name one task, press start, and run a calm 10- or 25-minute focus ring with a local session count. **Selected.** Reference: [Refocus](https://refocus.run/).
5. **Client-time overlap rail** — slide through the day to find a working overlap between Bogota and a selected client city. Reference: [World Time Buddy](https://www.worldtimebuddy.com/).
6. **Pick the next move** — enter up to five small tasks, then spin a restrained decision wheel when stuck. Reference: [Wheel of Names](https://wheelofnames.com/).
7. **Two-option decision compass** — score two choices against three editable criteria; the better option gently takes visual weight. Reference: [DecTrack decision matrix](https://dectrack.com/en/templates/decision-matrix).
8. **Study planner** — place subjects or tasks into a compact Mon–Fri study grid and make overload obvious. **Selected.** Reference: [Hourframe study planner](https://hourframe.com/).
9. **Deadline buffer meter** — select effort, deadline, and available sessions; a simple visual buffer turns reassuring, tight, or impossible. Reference: [ChronoClock project estimator](https://chronoclock.app/project-estimator).
10. **Meeting mini-agenda** — add three agenda items, give each a time share, and watch a 30-minute meeting bar fill up. Reference direction: [Toggl Planner](https://toggl.com/features/online-calendar-planner).
11. **Project handoff checklist** — check deliverables such as responsive QA, accessibility pass, assets, and handoff notes; completion becomes a useful visual seal. Reference: [DayWeaver demo](https://day-weaver.com/demo).
12. **Brain-dump sorter** — add short notes, then flick each into Build, Research, or Later. It demonstrates direct manipulation without becoming a notes app. Reference: [Kanboard live demo](https://kanboard.io/kanban-board-online).
13. **Daily energy allocator** — start with five energy points and spend them on deep work, admin, calls, and learning; it makes over-planning tangible. Reference: [DayWeaver demo](https://day-weaver.com/demo).
14. **Keyboard micro-drill** — one prompt at a time for a Figma, browser, or VS Code shortcut; accept the real key combination and track a local streak. Reference: [ShortcutFoo](https://www.shortcutfoo.com/).
15. **Reading queue compass** — add a title, then position it by effort and curiosity; the closest card becomes the next thing to read. This is a compact personal decision aid, not a bookmark manager. Reference: [The StoryGraph](https://app.thestorygraph.com/).
16. **Break the brief** — pick audience, goal, and constraint chips; a one-sentence project brief builds live beneath them. Useful for client/discovery thinking and visibly interactive. Reference: [Digital Project Brief Builder](https://solutionsmhe.com/en/tools/project-brief-builder).
17. **Scope slider** — move a single control from quick to robust; the card reveals the trade-offs in deliverables, timeline, and risk. It makes product judgment visible. Reference: [Project Scope Builder](https://marquefactory.com/tools/project-scope-builder/).
18. **Component state cockpit** — switch a small product card through empty, loading, error, success, and filled states. Keep it tactile and code-minded. Reference: [Storybook](https://storybook.js.org/).
19. **Palette recall** — flash a small design palette, then rebuild the color order from memory; keep the feedback clean and local. Reference: [Color Game](https://color.method.ac/).
20. **Mood-to-motion selector** — choose words such as calm, sharp, playful, or dense; the card changes easing, duration, and spacing to show motion-system judgment. Reference: [cubic-bezier.com](https://cubic-bezier.com/).

## Selection Rule

Prefer ideas that have one understandable action, persistent local state when it is useful, and a visible result within a single homepage card. Do not build full versions of established tools just to mimic them.
