# About rebuild: research, thesis evidence, and process selection bank

Date: 19 October 2026. Status: read-only research and draft methods, not an implementation or claim of Felipe's current professional process.

## Scope and method

Inventoried all 163 open Chrome tabs, including tabs outside the About group and inside collapsed groups. Reviewed the portfolio/studio pages throughout that inventory, followed relevant About/services/process links, and sampled applied case studies. This is not a claim that every document behind every open commercial/admin/library tab was read, or that every linked project on every portfolio was exhaustively audited.

Unrelated work/admin tabs, account pages, retailer sites, component libraries, TTS tools, Reddit searches, analytics products, and Awwwards collection indexes were classified from the inventory rather than treated as personal design-process sources. No account data was submitted and no user tabs were closed or rearranged. Browser-based reading supplemented web extraction where text extraction failed.

The positive list below separates an explicit personal workflow, a general methodology resource, an applied case study, and a general philosophy. They should not be conflated.

## Thesis located and read

File: `C:\OLDPC\Tesis\DiseñoAutodidacta-AutomóvilPersonal-201914388.pdf`

Title: Diseño Autodidacta: Automóvil Personal.

Author: Juan Felipe Salazar Trujillo. Public portfolio name remains Felipe Salazar.

Institution: Universidad de los Andes, Faculty of Architecture and Design. Cover date: Bogotá, November 2023. A later file modification date does not change the document's academic date or prove the graduation date.

95 pages. Read the abstract, introduction, motivation, process, key research/reframing sections, development, limitations, final outcome, and conclusions. Visually reviewed pages 46, 55 and 56. The full technical literature review and every appendix were not independently fact-checked. Regulatory/engineering passages are historical project research, not present-day legal or engineering advice.

### Findings with page references

- Pages 13–15: the central question is whether a broadly trained designer can transfer their knowledge into a new specialty. Automotive styling and curiosity motivate the project.
- Pages 33–34: his chosen sequence is discover, define, ideate, prototype, with iteration. He explicitly references Design Thinking, Lean, Double Diamond and IDEO. A list of methods discussed in the literature is not proof each was executed.
- Pages 41–42: initial audience exploration used surveys and perceptual maps; the document states 60 respondents. This is academic research context, not commercial UX-project evidence.
- Pages 43–44: after reflection and tutor feedback, he identified a mismatch between a broadly Colombian audience and the original purpose of a personal car. He separated useful research from unnecessary research, redefined the user as himself, and returned to discovery. This is concrete evidence of reframing rather than a tidy linear story.
- Pages 44–49: moodboard, word cloud, self-based persona, brand identity prism, perceptual/radar maps, brief, sketches, dimensions and drawings.
- Pages 50–51: 2D rendering, occupant packaging, Rhinoceros 3D work and further design changes. The text states that the 3D result was conceptual, not production-ready. Time and the learning curve prevented the planned physical prototype.
- Pages 55–57: Zarllo Cielo, a personal electric coupe concept, with Art Nouveau/Bauhaus influences and feline anatomy references. Its name is connected to his cat. Keep it a concept; no manufactured-vehicle claim.
- Page 59: conclusions emphasize multidisciplinarity, adapting methods, early course correction, and balancing ambition against technical/time constraints.

### Best lesson for the About page

The strongest story is the decision to change the brief after discovering the initial audience framing did not fit the project's purpose. It makes curiosity, self-awareness and iteration tangible. A short About callout can lead to a later case study; a full thesis case study is not part of this approval draft.

## Framework comparison

These approaches overlap. Some are process maps, some are team cadences, and others are specific testing methods. They do not belong in one claim that Felipe “uses everything.”

| Framework or approach | What it organizes | Fit to evidence and proposed use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Double Diamond | Discover, define, develop, deliver, with expansion and narrowing | Strongest documented reference from thesis. A useful backbone, not proof of a rigid routine. |
| Design Thinking | Understanding needs, defining the challenge, exploring, prototyping, testing, implementing | Thesis explicitly cites it. Phase counts differ by author; NN/g includes implementation in its six-phase model. |
| IDEO human-centred practice | Flexible movement from inspiration to ideas and making/implementation | Explicit thesis reference. Treat as an influence, not a certification. |
| Human/user-centred design | Involving and learning about people throughout the work | Broader orientation. Confirm actual participant access and current practices. |
| Lean UX | Assumptions, experiments, short learning loops | Thesis mentions it. Professional application still to confirm. |
| Agile UX / Scrum / Kanban | Organizing collaborative work and delivery while research/design continue | Team-working context, not a replacement for UX inquiry. No professional use claimed yet. |
| Dual-track discovery/delivery | Learning what to build while building validated work | Plausible match for design/code closeness, but unconfirmed. Both kinds of work involve the team. |
| Design Sprint | Time-boxed alignment, alternatives, decisions, prototype and validation | Optional for a bounded question; do not assume it is his ordinary process. Google and five-day variants use different phase labels. |
| RITE | Test, change a clear issue quickly, verify with further participants | A particular iterative testing approach. Ask whether he has actually worked this way. |
| Sequential / stage-gated work | Planned handoffs and approved phases | A possible client context, especially fixed requirements. Do not label his current job Waterfall without evidence. |
| Systems/service design | Relationships and experience across channels and organizations | Optional broader work, not an established specialty. |
| Goal-oriented design / jobs-to-be-done | Goals and desired progress that guide decisions | A framing lens rather than an entire delivery workflow. |
| Brand strategy to identity/web | Positioning, visual exploration, identity system and digital application | Relevant to confirmed branding history; exact routine remains to select. |
| Creative coding / technical experimentation | Sketch, prototype in code, judge interaction, refine and test constraints | Relevant learning/interest; method specifics need confirmation. |

Primary and authoritative sources:

- [Design Council: Double Diamond](https://www.designcouncil.org.uk/resources/the-double-diamond/). Iteration and returning to the problem are explicit; it is not an instruction manual for every project.
- [NN/g: Design Thinking 101](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/design-thinking/). Six phases and flexible, cyclical use.
- [IDEO: Is design thinking a set, step-by-step process?](https://designthinking.ideo.com/faq/isnt-design-thinking-a-set-step-by-step-process). Context-dependent use rather than a universal recipe.
- [Google Design Sprint Kit: Methodology](https://designsprintkit.withgoogle.com/methodology). Understand, define, sketch, decide, prototype, validate.
- [NN/g: Lean UX & Agile study guide](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/lean-ux-agile-study-guide/). Distinguishes sequential, adaptive and experimental approaches and connects ongoing research to delivery.
- [Jeff Patton: Dual-track development](https://jpattonassociates.com/dual-track-development/). Two kinds of work, not separate teams or a one-time discovery phase.
- [Original RITE method paper](https://jpattonassociates.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/04/rite_method.pdf). Rapid evaluation, fixing and retesting. The publisher's later file date is not the origin date of the method.
- [Design Council: Systemic Design Framework](https://www.designcouncil.org.uk/resources/systemic-design-framework/). Wider systems and relationships.

## Draft method bank for Felipe to select from

This is deliberately broad, not literally every method ever devised. It covers the main UX/UI, branding, web-development and creative-technology branches relevant to the interview. It is NOT public first-person copy. For each applicable item mark P = professional use, A = academic use, L = learning/interest only, N = not used. Mark other methods not listed as well. A single project rarely needs all these activities.

### 1. Frame the work

Project brief; stakeholder conversations; business/user goals; constraints and assumptions; scope and exclusions; success criteria; current-state audit; content inventory; risk prioritization; technical feasibility discussion; delivery plan; responsibilities/decision owner.

### 2. Learn about people and context

Desk research; competitive/comparative review; domain research; user interviews; contextual inquiry/observation; surveys; diary studies; existing support/feedback review; analytics/funnel review; search-log review; heatmap/session-recording review with appropriate privacy safeguards; accessibility research; participatory/co-design workshops.

### 3. Synthesize and define

Affinity mapping; thematic analysis; research summaries; evidence-based personas; provisional proto-personas clearly labelled as assumptions; empathy maps; journey maps; service blueprints; jobs-to-be-done; problem statements; how-might-we questions; opportunity mapping; requirements; prioritization; research-to-decision traceability.

### 4. Structure the experience

Information architecture; sitemap; content hierarchy; card sorting; tree testing; task flows; user flows; wireflows; story mapping; navigation models; content modelling; UX writing; low-fidelity wireframes; mobile/content order; states and edge-case mapping.

### 5. Explore directions

Sketching; brainstorming; Crazy 8s; design studios; parallel alternatives; storyboards; moodboards; visual references; concept comparison; critique; collaborative workshops; concept testing; brand positioning; identity prism; perceptual maps; art-direction explorations.

### 6. Make it tangible

Paper prototypes; clickable prototypes; high-fidelity UI; responsive layouts; component libraries; tokens/design systems; interaction specifications; motion studies; coded prototypes; 3D/blockout experiments; material/form exploration; visual identity systems; logo/typography/colour exploration; accessible content and component states.

### 7. Evaluate and revise

Moderated usability testing; unmoderated task testing; contextual prototype evaluation; heuristic review; cognitive walkthrough; accessibility checks; keyboard/screen-reader checks; concept/comprehension testing; design critique; RITE-style rapid fix/retest; preference tests with an appropriate question; quantitative task measurements; A/B experiments when sample size and setup support valid conclusions; performance/device checks; iteration and scope adjustment.

### 8. Implement, launch, learn

Developer collaboration; direct frontend implementation; documentation/specifications; semantic HTML; responsive/browser checks; CMS/content migration; component/state QA; accessibility QA; loading/error/empty states; performance optimization; basic technical SEO; launch checks; handover/training; feedback collection; post-launch analytics; maintenance; retrospective; backlog of improvements.

### Cross-cutting ways of working

Solo vs collaborative; async vs workshops; fixed scope vs iterative scope; project-based vs ongoing product work; new product vs redesign vs migration; discovery before commitment vs discovery alongside delivery; prototype-led vs content-first vs code-first exploration; AI-assisted research synthesis/ideation/code with source checking and data safeguards; regular critique; pairing with developers; check-ins with stakeholders; direct participant access vs constrained research.

These are choices to discuss, not a request to make the public page longer. The finished About should summarize the selected practice; the Process destination can explain it in depth after a separate content pass.
